Low balance notifications

The last piece of v2. The app already turns the tray amber and shows a callout on the dashboard, but if you are not looking at either, nothing reaches you, which is the whole point of polling in the background. Nothing here is applied to the app yet.

What it says

the OS draws the frame

Only the icon and two lines of text belong to Meterix. The toast frame, its buttons and where it appears are the operating system's, so this is an approximation of Windows 11 rather than a design we control. That means the copy has to do all the work on its own.

Why the third one matters more than it looks. A balance running low is something you can deal with tomorrow. A key that has been revoked or expired means the app has quietly stopped tracking that provider, and nothing on screen says so unless you open it. That is the failure this app exists to prevent, so it deserves a notification more than a low balance does.

When it fires

one check every 30 minutes

This is the part that decides whether the feature is useful or infuriating. The teal mark is the moment a balance drops below its threshold; dots are notifications.

Shown to be rejected, not as a real option. At a 30 minute poll that is 48 notifications a day per provider. Within an hour you would turn the feature off, which is worse than not having it.

The settings rows

one new zone

Two rows, sitting between Alerts and Startup on the settings screen. They use the same row shape as everything already there, so nothing new has to be invented.

Notify me when a balance drops below its threshold
Uses each provider's own threshold, so a provider you have set to $10 warns at $10.
While it stays low
What happens after the first notification, if you have not topped up. Silent by default, because the tray already stays amber until you do.
Tell me when a key stops working
Separate from the balance warning, because it means the app has stopped tracking that provider entirely.
Deliberately not included

A per-provider mute. It looks like it should exist, but the per-provider threshold already does the job: if you do not want to hear about a provider until it is nearly empty, set its threshold low. Adding a second control for the same intent gives you two places to look when a notification does not arrive.

What the app has to remember. "The moment it crosses" only works if the app knows it has already spoken. That state has to survive a restart, or closing and reopening the app re-fires a notification for a balance that has been low for days. One row per provider in the database, the same place the threshold lives.

Forecasts are not offered. The dashboard shows a blended burn rate, but it is summed across providers, so "about 3 days left" would be a per-provider claim built from a number that is not per provider. Doing it properly needs a per-provider burn rate first. Say the word if you want that in scope.

Quiet hours are not offered either. Waking someone at 03:00 because a balance crossed $2 is a real annoyance, but it is a second feature and the thresholds are already yours to set.
