Mockup — the app on one page

Meterix with six providers

The dashboard, the settings screen and the tray, all in one place, drawn with six providers instead of the two the app ships with. Everything below uses the real palette, the real type scale and the real geometry from app/src and app/src-tauri/src/main.rs, so it can be compared against the running app line by line.

The app cannot show six providers today. PROVIDERS in the core is ["openrouter", "cheaperinference"] — a fixed array of two. The extra four here are illustrative, to show what the layout does when the list grows, and two real problems fall out of it. Both are described at the bottom.

The popover's header dot follows the worst health across every provider, so with two under their threshold it is amber. Its per-row dots do not: each row shows failed ? copper : teal, so a provider that is merely low still gets a teal dot next to an amber header. The dashboard's own badge for this was removed on request, so the row dots are now the only place a low provider is not marked as low.

What six providers surfaces

needs a decision

1. The chart has two colours for any number of providers. App.tsx assigns tone: index === 0 ? "teal" : "copper", and BalanceChart knows four tones in total — teal, copper, amber and muted, where muted means "could not be read". So five providers produce one teal line and four identical copper ones, and there is no way to tell which is which except by their end labels.

Fixing it means a categorical palette of at least six, kept clear of the three colours that already carry meaning — amber is "low", copper is "failed", teal is "healthy" — and legible on the dark panel. Something like:

Shown as swatches only, not drawn into the chart above, because nothing of the sort is built and picking six colours is a design decision rather than a repair. The four existing tones keep their meanings either way.

2. Six providers means six API calls every poll. fetch_balances walks PROVIDERS and fetches each one, so the interval does not change but the work scales with the list. At 30 minutes that is 288 requests a day rather than 96, and one slow provider holds up the batch. A per-provider interval, or a concurrency limit, is not built.

3. Two windows cost six WebView2 processes. tauri.conf.json declares main and tray, and the second is a live hidden webview from startup whether or not the popover is ever opened. Measured, that is ~403 MB of working set, of which 29 MB is this app and 374 MB is the Edge runtime.

None of these three are regressions — the app runs correctly with the two providers it has. They are what the layout and the process model do when the list grows, which is the reason for drawing it with six.
