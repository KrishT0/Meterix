Meterix, colour revamp

Two changes. Ten shades for the graph, so six providers can actually be told apart. And the cards become the background lifted one step and tinted with the provider's own colour, instead of a saturated block whose colour means something else.

The ten shades

new

Mid-lightness so no line dominates the others, and separated in hue so neighbours stay distinguishable at 1.75px. These are for identity — which provider a line, dot or card belongs to.

The set is searched rather than chosen by eye. Hues are spread evenly through the 280 degrees amber and copper do not own, then lightness was tuned per colour to hold every shade in the band the app's own accents sit in. Measured in CIELAB: closest pair ΔE 15.3, nearest to amber ΔE 43.5, nearest to copper ΔE 45.9, luminance 148–191 against the #131312 backdrop. For scale, ΔE 10 is the usual bar for "clearly different".

Amber, orange and copper are deliberately absent. Those three already carry meaning here — amber is "under your threshold", copper is "a key was rejected". A provider whose line happened to be amber would be unreadable. Teal stays in the palette as shade one, but health is no longer drawn with it, so there is no double meaning.

What cannot be had. Narrowing the reserved arc to buy more separation pulls a chartreuse shade to within ΔE 30.6 of the amber that means "under threshold", so the two would start to be confusable on a thin line. Ten distinct shades, an untouched amber arc and a comfortable tonal band are over-constrained for a dark interface — a search requiring all three returns nothing at all. This palette is the compromise, weighted toward keeping amber and copper unambiguous, because those two have to be readable at a glance in the tray icon where there is no label to fall back on.

Beyond about ten providers the answer stops being colour. Two providers sharing a shade with a distinguishing dash pattern, or filtering the chart to a few providers at a time, both beat adding an eleventh hue nobody can name.

The card

changed

The tint becomes the surface nudged toward the provider's hue — color-mix(in oklab, hue 7%, surface) — so it reads as a lighter, colour-tinged panel rather than a filled swatch. The hue also drives the border, the meter and the basis label, so one colour identifies the card in every place it appears.

Health moves out of the background and into a chip at the bottom of the card. That was the blockage before: the tint was the health signal, so it could not also be identity, and six providers meant six identical-looking panels.

What building this takes

not built

1. A palette in the theme. The ten shades become ten tokens in app/src/index.css, and each provider gets an index. PROVIDERS in the core is a fixed array of two today, so the fourth provider has no definition to take a hue from — the list has to become data before six is real.

2. One function instead of a ternary. App.tsx currently does tone: index === 0 ? "teal" : "copper". That becomes a lookup by index into the ten. BalanceChart's four-tone map grows to match, and its Tone type stops being the same type as health.

3. The card tint is derived, not declared. color-mix(in oklab, hue 7%, surface) needs the hue as a CSS variable on the card — a one-line style attribute, no per-state classes. It also removes the three hand-picked --teal-tint / --amber-tint / --copper-tint values, which is a deletion rather than an addition.

4. Health becomes a chip. The card, the table and the tray row each get the same small status element. The tray row is the one that changes most: today its dot is failed ? copper : teal, so a provider that is merely under its threshold shows a healthy dot. That is a bug this revamp fixes rather than introduces.

Nothing here changes the data model, the API or the polling. It is the presentation layer only: BalanceChart.tsx, ProviderCard.tsx, ProviderTable.tsx, TrayPopover.tsx, index.css, plus PROVIDERS becoming data if six providers is the goal rather than an illustration.
